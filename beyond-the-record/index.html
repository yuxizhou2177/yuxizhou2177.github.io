Record aquatic animal production, but availability remains uneven across regions

SOFIA 2026 reports a new high in global aquatic animal production, with aquaculture now accounting for 53% of the total, while per-capita availability in Africa remains less than half the global average.

Global aquatic animal production reached 195 million tonnes in 2024, with aquaculture now producing the majority for the first time — 103 million tonnes, or 53% of the total. Yet the regional picture remains uneven: in 2023, aquatic animal food availability averaged 9.1 kg per person in Africa, compared with a global average of 21.1 kg.

Key messages

  • Global aquatic animal production reached a record 195 million tonnes in 2024. Aquaculture surpassed 100 million tonnes for the first time and now accounts for 53 percent of total production.
  • Aquatic animal foods continue to make a major contribution to food security and nutrition. 89 percent of production is used for human consumption, and aquatic foods provide at least one-fifth of animal protein intake for 3.1 billion people.

Issues to raise

  • Scaling up Blue Transformation to deliver not only higher production, but also better nutrition, livelihoods and resilience.
  • Supporting sustainable aquaculture, effective fisheries management and stronger implementation of the Small-Scale Fisheries Guidelines.

Watch points

  • Record production should not be equated with resolved sustainability challenges. Capture fisheries production has largely plateaued.
  • Access remains uneven across regions. Per capita aquatic animal food availability remains well below the global average in Africa and Latin America and the Caribbean, and availability does not necessarily reflect actual consumption.

Source: FAO. 2026. The State of World Fisheries and Aquaculture 2026 — Blue Transformation: turning vision into impact. Rome. Read the full report →
